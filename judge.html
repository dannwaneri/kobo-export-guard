Judge path · 3 minutes

No account, no key. The data is synthetic: 40 made-up households.

1 The normal run (desktop Chrome with Gemini Nano)

Open the app with the sample loaded

  1. Step 2 shows each column and the rule that decided it: metadata such as username, deviceid, _submission_time is dropped; names, phones and household IDs are dropped; GPS is rounded.
  2. Step 3: press Scan with Gemini Nano on this device. Names and places in the comments are found in the browser; phones by code.
  3. Step 4: review the masks. Step 5: download the cleaned CSV and the receipt.
  4. Watch the AI requests sent counter in the header: it stays at 0 through the scan.

2 The refusal (a browser without the built-in model)

Open the app as if Nano were missing

This removes LanguageModel before the page runs, like Firefox, Safari or a phone. In that case the Firebase AI Logic SDK (13.4.0) sends even ONLY_ON_DEVICE calls to the cloud. This app checks first and refuses: the scan button is disabled, the code-only scan still works, and the counter stays at 0.

3 The one cloud call

Step 6 sends only the cleaned comments to Gemini in the cloud for a narrative summary, after consent and a final code check that no phone, email or masked name remains. The counter then shows exactly one request.

Why this exists

An NGO data officer told us: raw Kobo exports go to partners “more often than our data protection policy says”; cleaning is done by hand; the comments column, half-cleaned GPS and forgotten metadata are what leak; and staff paste comment rows into chatbots to write reports.
